The words this project leans on, sorted by where they come from, so that a reader can tell a dictionary word from a term of art, and a term of art from a word coined here.

As commonly accepted

⚜︎
Ceremony. A fixed form, followed for its own sake and for what it keeps whole: a greeting, a thanks, a farewell.
☮︎
Conciliation. Bringing two parties back to good terms after a difference.
≡
Convention. An agreed way of doing a thing, kept because others keep it too. In language, every word is one.
⇄
Interchange. A giving and taking in turn. Conversation, at its plainest.
⊢
Premise. What is set down first, for the rest to follow from. From Latin praemissa, "sent before."
❧
Proposition. A putting-forth: something set before another for consideration. In logic, a statement that is true or false.
☙
Tact. A sense of what will spare another trouble, and the habit of acting on it.

As used in linguistic and communication science

☷
Common ground. What both parties know they share, and know the other knows. Herbert Clark.
☞
Declarative pointing. Pointing only so that another will look too, and then checking that they did: "hey, come see this." Children do it from about a year old, and Michael Tomasello counts it a root of joint attention.
◓
Face. The public self a person claims in an exchange. Erving Goffman, and Penelope Brown and Stephen Levinson.
↺
Feedback. A return channel from receiver to sender. It adds no capacity to a channel without memory, but makes reliable sending simpler. Claude Shannon.
⊥
Grounding. The work two parties do, turn by turn, to make sure each has understood the other well enough for the purpose at hand. Herbert Clark and Susan Brennan.
⇌
Handshake. The opening exchange in which two machines agree how to talk: what each can do, and at what speed. In fax, the receiver states its abilities, the sender proposes a mode, and the receiver confirms or asks for less (ITU T.30).
∴
Implicature. What a speaker means beyond what the words say, which the hearer infers. H. P. Grice.
↷
Indirect speech act. A request or offer made in the form of something else: "Could you pass the salt?" John Searle.
✿
Information scent. The cues that tell a reader whether a path is worth following. Peter Pirolli and Stuart Card.
△
Joint attention. Two people attending to one thing, each knowing the other attends. Michael Tomasello.
☍
Metacommunication. Communication about the communication: the signal that says how to take a message, as "this is play." Jurgen Ruesch and Gregory Bateson.
☼
Phatic communion. Speech whose purpose is the bond, not the news. Bronisław Malinowski, and Roman Jakobson's phatic function, which concerns the channel itself.
⁂
Redundancy. The share of a message not needed to fix its meaning, which lets it survive noise. Ordinary English is about half redundant. Claude Shannon.
◔
Successive refinement. Sending a coarse description first and finer ones after. For many sources, the wait costs nothing. William Equitz and Thomas Cover.

Nuage shop-talk

✺
Computational impressionism. Letting a large model and Nuage leave impressions on each other across many conversations, until what the model knows recrystallizes on the deterministic side, in few bytes. A goal, not yet a method.
⊕
Consense. The shared sense two or more parties arrive at by many small exchanges. Rarer and humbler than consensus.
⊚
Epiring. A ring that turns about the spine of a conversation and feeds it forward. Nuage has two. The listening ring keeps what a pupil supposes when Nuage cannot answer it ("Do clouds form when warm air rises?"), in plain words, in its notes. The teaching ring asks a larger model for one card that would answer, proves it, and leaves it for a person's ruling. Nothing a pupil says enters the cloud unruled. User1's word.
⚘︎
Consettle. To settle together, into place, as barbs on a feather or summaries in a thatch.
iiii
Coopsys. Four eyes reading together: many eyes on one shared thing, each knowing the others see it. Not a firing line but "hey, come see this." User1's word, and its sigil.
≋
Diffractive gratiation. Thought against thought, as light through a grating: nothing added, but each crossing shows the colours that were in both.
⇋
Intercorroborate. To confirm one another's sense, back and forth, until it holds.
✧
Interscintillation. A conversation in sparks, as between two boats by Aldis lamp, each word answered with a flash. From Latin scintilla, a spark.
◐
Mimia. A mirror engine that knows nothing, ground against a model's answers until it shows the model's manner, and perhaps its maker. Balanced ternary and nested, so any prefix of her file is a smaller Mimia. She yields to Nuage's cards on what Nuage knows. User1's character, from the imaginal Exaspera: she can make every face, and by definition can never be reached.
◈
Munus. Latin for a gift, a duty, an office. Here, the thing exchanged in every turn of talk, at the root of "communication."
▢
Onto-box. The thin rounded ring around a word Nuage knows as a kind with kinds of its own, and around a citation it stands on.
♪︎♪︎
Patty-cake. Nuage's sparring with a language model: questions, conversations, a pupil, cards, and a person's ruling.
↗
Pro-poser. One who places before. The lightest imposition upon the voluntary interest of another.
◉
Pupil. A language model playing a curious school pupil, who picks a topic and talks with Nuage, then says whether it was satisfied.
✦
Scintil. One spark of an interscintillation: a single small acknowledgement that narrows the error between minds.
☰
Shelf. Knowledge kept apart in its own block and read in only when first asked about, as Flags of Our World.
◎
Spinnetry. The holiness of what is turned about an axis: the ring, the bowl, the bell.
♡
Sympathesis. The shared feeling in which a conversation takes place. A proposition carries no force for its medium to lose, so it travels whole.
⋀
Thatch, thatched consettlement. A plain-text summary of compressed content: a title, and two spaces in, what lies below, so that a reader knows whether to unpack it.
|||
The third plate. The party whose presence keeps two others from agreeing in a shared error, as in Whitworth's three-plate method.
